## 1. Evidence Objective

Document a simulated security control weakness involving inconsistent multifactor authentication (MFA) requirements and identify the evidence needed to assess and remediate the finding.

This record uses fictional information for educational purposes. No real organization, user accounts, or authentication systems were examined.

## 2. Simulated Policy Evidence

**Document:** Sample Authentication and Access Control Policy  
**Evidence Reference:** MFA-E01  
**Document Status:** Fictional policy excerpt

### Sample Policy Excerpt

> Employees must use a unique username and password to access organizational systems. Multifactor authentication is required for remote administrative access. MFA requirements for other workforce applications are determined by individual system administrators.

This policy excerpt was created for the simulation. It is not an actual organizational policy.

## 3. Control Gap Identified

The fictional policy does not consistently require MFA across all applicable workforce accounts and applications.

**Simulated finding:** MFA enforcement gap  
**Control Area:** Identity and Access Management  
**Assessment Result:** Gap Identified  
**Finding Status:** Open

**Potential consequences:**
- Unauthorized access following compromised passwords.
- Exposure of sensitive patient information.
- Increased risk of account takeover.
- Disruption of healthcare operations.

These consequences are potential risks, not confirmed incidents.

## 4. Evidence Assessment

| Assessment Question | Simulated Observation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Is MFA required for remote administrative access? | Yes, according to the fictional policy | Partially Addressed |
| Is MFA required for all applicable workforce accounts? | No consistent requirement is documented | Gap Identified |
| Are MFA exceptions formally approved? | No exception process is described | Not Established |
| Are MFA settings independently verified? | No configuration testing was performed | Not Tested |
| Are authentication logs available for review? | No logs were collected | Not Verified |

## 5. Recommended Corrective Actions

1. Identify accounts and applications requiring MFA.
2. Establish consistent MFA requirements for applicable accounts.
3. Prefer phishing-resistant MFA methods where supported.
4. Document and approve necessary exceptions.
5. Provide MFA enrollment and security awareness training.
6. Review authentication logs and investigate suspicious activity.
7. Validate MFA enforcement through authorized testing.
