# Executive Summary
## Cybersecurity Risk Assessment and Risk Register

**Organization:** Clearview Community Health (Fictional)  
**Assessment Type:** Simulated Cybersecurity Risk Assessment  
**Primary Focus:** Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)  
**Prepared by:** Wendell Jean-Pierre

### 1. Executive Overview

This project presents a simulated cybersecurity risk assessment for Clearview Community Health, a fictional healthcare organization with approximately 75 employees operating across two clinic locations.

The assessment examines cybersecurity risks involving patient information, employee accounts, clinical systems, software vulnerabilities, and third-party vendors.

The objective is to identify and prioritize risks, recommend appropriate security controls, and document proposed remediation activities.

All findings, evidence, and organizational details are fictional and intended for professional portfolio development.

### 2. Risk Assessment Methodology

Five cybersecurity risks were evaluated using a qualitative scoring methodology.

Each risk received a likelihood score from 1 to 5 and an impact score from 1 to 5.

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

The project uses the following scoring thresholds:

- **Low:** 1–4
- **Medium:** 5–9
- **High:** 10–15
- **Critical:** 16–25

These thresholds were defined for this simulated assessment and are not an official NIST scoring scale.

### 3. Summary of Identified Risks

| Risk ID | Risk Description | Score | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | Phishing compromises employee accounts | 16 | Critical |
| R-02 | Unauthorized access to patient records | 15 | High |
| R-03 | Ransomware disrupts clinical systems | 15 | High |
| R-04 | Unpatched systems expose vulnerabilities | 16 | Critical |
| R-05 | Third-party vendor exposes patient information | 15 | High |

**Assessment Results:**

- Total risks identified: 5
- Critical risks: 2
- High risks: 3
- Medium risks: 0
- Low risks: 0

All five scenarios require documented risk treatment consideration, with the two Critical risks receiving the highest proposed priority.

### 4. Recommended Security Controls

The following controls are recommended for the fictional organization:

1. **Identity and Access Security:** Strengthen multifactor authentication (MFA), email filtering, and security awareness training.
2. **Patient Information Protection:** Apply role-based access controls, audit logging, and periodic access reviews.
3. **Ransomware Resilience:** Maintain protected backups, endpoint security, and tested incident response procedures.
4. **Vulnerability Management:** Establish patch management schedules, vulnerability scanning, and remediation deadlines.
5. **Third-Party Risk Management:** Conduct vendor security reviews, assess contractual safeguards, and document vendor-related risks.

These are proposed recommendations. Their implementation and effectiveness have not been verified.

### 5. Remediation Priorities

**Priority 1: Critical Risks**

- R-01: Reduce phishing-related account compromise risk through stronger authentication and email security controls.
- R-04: Reduce exposure from unpatched systems through formal vulnerability and patch management.

**Priority 2: High Risks**

- R-02: Improve protection against unauthorized access to patient records.
- R-03: Strengthen ransomware prevention, recovery readiness, and incident response.
- R-05: Improve vendor risk assessment and third-party security governance.

Proposed risk owners should review these priorities, confirm risk acceptance criteria, and determine appropriate treatment plans.

### 6. Assessment Limitations and Current Status

This project is a simulated GRC case study. No live production systems were assessed, no actual patient information was accessed, and no security controls were independently tested.

The supporting MFA evidence and remediation documentation describe hypothetical findings and proposed corrective actions.

Remediation activities remain open in the simulated tracker. No completed remediation or verified control effectiveness is claimed.

### 7. Executive Conclusion

The simulated assessment identified five cybersecurity risks that could affect confidentiality, integrity, and availability in a healthcare environment.

The recommended next steps are to validate the risk scenarios, confirm accountable risk owners, assess existing controls, approve remediation priorities, and establish evidence-based verification criteria.

This project demonstrates a structured approach to cybersecurity risk identification, scoring, prioritization, control recommendations, and remediation tracking using concepts informed by NIST guidance and healthcare security requirements.
